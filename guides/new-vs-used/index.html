Car buying guide

New vs. used: which one is right for you?

The honest answer: if the numbers work, buy new. If they don’t, buy used from someone you trust. Here is how to tell which one you are — and either way, Gus sells both, so you will get the straight answer, not a sales pitch.

Why new is usually the better buy

A new car comes with a full factory warranty from day one — Hyundai backs its new vehicles with a 10-year / 100,000-mile powertrain limited warranty — the latest safety tech, no unknown history, and you pick the exact car: the color, the features, all of it. Financing is often cheaper on new cars too. If the monthly payment fits your budget, new is the easiest car to own, and it is the one Gus will usually steer you toward.

When used is the smart call

Used costs less to buy and less per month, and someone else already took the biggest depreciation hit. A certified used Hyundai gets inspected and carries extra coverage, so it is not a gamble the way a random used car can be. A good used car from the right place beats a new car you cannot afford — and Gus would rather put you in a used car that fits than watch you stretch for a new one that doesn’t.

The middle ground: certified used

Hyundai Certified Used vehicles are inspected before they go on the lot and carry additional warranty coverage. It is the closest thing to new without the new price — a good middle ground if new is just out of reach.

How to decide in five minutes

Ask yourself four things: what monthly payment can you live with, including insurance? How long will you keep the car? Do you drive a lot — because warranty coverage matters more the more you drive? And do you need the latest safety features, for kids or for peace of mind? Text Gus your budget and he will tell you which side of the line you are on — new or used — before you ever set foot in the store.
