Car buying guide

Trading in your car? Here’s how it works.

Your current car is worth something — usually more than people expect — and trading it in is the simplest way to put that value toward your next one. Here is the whole process, with no surprises.

How a trade-in works

You bring the car in, Gus looks it over, and he gives you a number. That number comes straight off the price of the car you are buying — you only pay the difference. In Illinois, the trade-in value also comes off the price before sales tax is figured, so you only pay tax on the difference too.

If your car is worth more than the car you are buying, the difference comes back to you. And if you still owe money on your trade, Gus will walk you through exactly how that works — no guessing.

What makes your trade worth more

A car that runs, drives, and is cleaned out is worth more than the same car dirty and full of stuff. Wash it, clear out the trunk and glove box, and bring both keys if you have them. Service records help if you kept them. And be straight about the dents and the noises — Gus will find them anyway, and honesty gets you a real number faster.

You don’t have to buy new to trade in

A trade-in works toward a used car too. If a used car is the right call for your budget, Gus will find you a good one — a used sale beats no sale, and he would rather put you in the right car than the wrong one. The trade-in still counts the same way.

The best moment to go new

If you are trading in, it is usually the best moment to buy new: full factory warranty from day one, the latest safety tech, no unknown history, and you pick the exact car — color, features, all of it. Hyundai backs its new vehicles with a 10-year / 100,000-mile powertrain limited warranty, so you are covered long after the trade is forgotten.
